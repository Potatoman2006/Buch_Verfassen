# The Aurelian Court Isles

The Aurelian Isles rise from southern water in terraces of dark volcanic stone, white plaster, aromatic scrub, gardens, and observatory towers. At night their lamps climb the hills like a second arrangement of stars. They are a kingdom of island courts whose sovereign must be accepted by houses proud of learning and no less proud of their ancestry. Beauty here is cultivated, argued over, and often made possible by people whose names never reach the court.

## Court islands and ash villages

On Auriel, human, Aelri, Serev, and Ruun households work in schools, gardens, workshops, fisheries, and the royal residence. Thalassic is common, with a formal court register prized by some families and mocked by others. The smaller island of Veyru is rougher: volcanic soil, goat paths, fishing coves, and villages rebuilt after Ash Summer. Its residents keep polished black stones from lost houses beside their hearths.

Families eat flatbreads, beans, fish, bitter greens, goat cheese, and fruit from irrigated terraces. Children learn the names of visible stars alongside household work, but an education's length depends on who can spare their labour. Public evening meals fill stepped courtyards during the cooling hours. Court festivals feature masked astronomical dances; village versions make the planets behave like quarrelling neighbours.

## The eclipse succession

Sovereign Ilyane Auriel has named her adopted Serev daughter, Eset, heir. A branch of the old house supports the sovereign's human nephew, claiming that adoption cannot carry the oldest island rites. Eset grew up inside those rites and finds the objection less sacred than convenient. Her rival is not wholly pleased to be used as a bloodline's answer to an actual person.

A coming predicted eclipse gives the dispute a public focus. The Dawn Path treats prediction as evidence of disciplined observation; a popular preacher says the darkened sun will reveal whom the heavens reject. The claim is belief, and a dangerous one: factions can arrange violence beneath an omen and call the result divine judgement. Eset wants the ceremony open to ordinary islanders. Her protectors fear that generosity will give an assassin a crowd.

Veyru asks the court for families, boats, and animals lost in the aftermath of Ash Summer to be remembered in the ceremony. Richer islands prefer a triumph about recovery. The quarrel concerns whose suffering fits the public story of the realm.

## Dawn and the dark underneath

Dawn Path teachers bring pupils outdoors to compare prediction with the actual sky. Devotional songs welcome correction as a kind of humility. That ideal does not stop a celebrated teacher from defending a bad conclusion. Lamp houses shelter travellers and children sent to study; the Choir of Tides blesses returning fishers. Families can share these practices without agreeing about which priest has the best understanding of the heavens.

In Veyru's ruined lava gardens, heat trapped beneath stone keeps flowers alive through cool months. Sulphurous vents sometimes kill birds and make a beautiful hollow unsafe. Islanders leave bowls of water for the birds on higher ground. Reverence can be an ordinary act that acknowledges a place's danger without denying its beauty.

## The observatories and the Starwell

Observation depends on clear skies, lenses, mathematics, and repeated work. Magic helps shape existing light through prepared glass and polished surfaces; it cannot read future choices from stars. At the Starwell, an Ilyran chamber lined with dark reflective mineral, a maintained pattern displays slowly moving arrangements of remembered light. Some correspond to present constellations; others do not. Scholars disagree whether they depict a different season, another latitude, artistic invention, or a sky much older than the chamber.

The chamber's effect is real and its meaning unresolved. Pilgrims kneel beneath its turning lights. Eset's teacher wants a long expedition beyond familiar southern waters to test one interpretation, while the court wants the chamber reserved for legitimising the succession. Knowledge becomes political because it can reveal a world far larger than a dynasty's claim.

## Places, neighbours, and stories

Auriel's Palace of Open Roofs joins courtyards where rain reaches carved drains shaped as fish. Veyru's Black Orchard grows beside houses buried to their lintels. The Starwell stands inland, requiring a climb through terraces whose workers rarely receive invitations to formal demonstrations. The Western Steps are crowded with foreign students, their cooking fires scenting the harbour differently from the palace kitchens.

Nine Beacons pilots test Aurelian predictions against weather that does not care who holds an academic chair. Reef Crown observers know current and breeding changes the court once dismissed. Velisara carries visiting scholars and court gossip in the same ships. Each neighbour can puncture the islands' certainty while depending on their learning.

An adopted heir must decide whether to accept a humiliating compromise to prevent civil war. A village teacher accompanies pupils to the eclipse and recognizes preparations for a killing. An elderly observer begins an expedition her institution has delayed throughout her life. A fisher's child understands a changing current better than an honoured guest and must find a way to be heard. A court dancer is asked to turn a sacred performance into a public declaration against someone she loves.
