# The Duchy of Merenne

Merenne lies between a wet western forest and the sea, a country of orchard slopes, rain-filled gorges, walled harbours, ruined coastal chapels, and headlands where white flowers grow in the mouths of old cannon. Fruit blossom makes spring villages look briefly innocent. Autumn reveals cider smoke, salt-burned trees, and the knowledge that a family can do everything well and still lose its roof to weather. Merenne's warmth is the warmth of neighbours who know precisely how much help costs.

## Orchard, harbour, and verge

The central orchard country grows apples, pears, nuts, grapes in sheltered districts, and oats or beans between young trees. Beekeepers travel with hives. Grafting skills pass through women and men, apprentices and fostered children, without always following land ownership. Harvest meals use long boards carried outdoors; someone always tells the same story about a pear too large to be real. Winter households dry fruit, mend clothes, and learn which neighbour has quietly stopped coming to shared suppers.

The ports face outward. Rookharbour and larger southern harbours have Merennic-speaking humans alongside Ruun, Kharad, Aelri, Urven, and visiting sailors from farther shores. They eat fish, bread, greens, beans, and fruit, with foreign seasonings when someone returns from a voyage. Port children can identify distant flags yet fear a dark inland path. Forest-edge children may know six edible fungi and regard a ship as a precarious thing to trust with one's life.

At the verge, Merennic overlaps with Bough tongues in mixed households. Forest and orchard customs meet in practical compromises: a tree may be coppiced for fuel while the spring below it remains undisturbed; a child may receive both a Lamp blessing and a Root naming meal. Neither makes the family halfway between authentic cultures. That combination is its own inherited life.

## A child duchess and an old quarrel

Duchess Sera Ordel is eleven at baseline. Her father's death left a regency divided between Lady Maelin Ordel, who favours coastal defence, and Lord Corven, who wants armed patrols deeper into the western forest. Both claim to preserve Sera's future. Maelin fears another storm killing people behind weakened walls. Corven fears the duchy becoming unable to protect villages against armed woodcutters, raiders, and predatory creatures at the verge. His preferred answer would also strengthen his personal command.

House Nhaleth and other Seven Bough communities dispute the cutting of ancient slopes for sea-wall and ship timber. Merenne's harbour families hear refusal as indifference to drowning children. Forest families hear urgency as the same excuse used every generation to take a little more. Recent mudslides have made the forest argument concrete. Raids against timber camps could still kill workers with little power over either side's decisions.

Sera is learning which adults answer a child's direct question honestly. That does not give her secret maturity or immediate command. Her ordinary wishes—privacy, friendship, a day without ceremonial clothes—coexist with a future others are already arranging through marriage, war, and fear.

## Saints, roots, and the uneasy dead

Saint Orra, the historical healer of the first century after Saltfall, is especially loved here. Stories remember her washing strangers' feet and arguing that an exhausted rescuer also needs shelter. The Covenant of Lamps maintains sickrooms and traveller beds in her name. Rival chapters disagree over dangerous fugitives: refuge is a duty, but so is protecting those already inside.

Root Communion rites place the first fallen blossom in running water and leave certain winter fruit for animals. Old coastal families also sing to the sea dead at empty tables. A priest may object to the wording and join the meal anyway. Beliefs that drowned relatives return as white birds remain beliefs; actual remembered-form echoes repeat sensory fragments near damaged old sites without restoring a person.

Vampires are known and feared, with no single ducal answer accepted everywhere. A rural family may conceal a turned relative while a harbour captain orders immediate pursuit. Shelter chapters insist on judging behaviour and protecting unwilling donors. The physical need for fresh sapient warm-blooded blood makes this a difficult practice, not a sentiment a compassionate speech resolves. Rookharbour has no established support network at baseline.

## The forest that answers in seasons

Merenne's western woods contain bell stags whose antlers resonate when they clash, crown owls that can take a lamb, and hearth drakes scavenging near charcoal camps. Most encounters are brief and ordinary. Near ancient wounds, rare stags carry living thorn growth among their antlers. Their shed growth can seed aggressive thickets where soil and water permit. They are dangerous animals, not speaking agents of a forest god.

The White Orchard grows over the ruins of an Ilyran funerary garden. Pale blossoms sometimes open together when a particular cold mist crosses the site. Local growth workers can study the interaction of old patterns, water, and living trees; they cannot command the whole wood. Families still picnic at its safe edge in season. Wonder and ordinary pleasure survive beside risk, which is why the place matters when a baron proposes fencing it for private hunting.

At Saint Orra's, a ruined monastery shelters old tidal chambers whose damaged patterns produce sensory echoes. The [Rookharbour corridor](story-ground.md) defines that site and its threat precisely. It is one small district's danger, not a secret explanation for all Merenne's grief or magic.

## Places and outward consequences

Ordel's Seawall faces a broad working harbour, with rooms in the ducal residence still stained by the Three Storms. Sable Orchard holds enormous old grafted trees bearing several related fruit varieties; a tree's survival can carry a family's history without holding its souls. The White Orchard draws pilgrims, botanists, lovers, and poachers for incompatible reasons. Briarbridge marks one lived edge of the forest, while Rookharbour connects that inland corridor to the western Inner Salt.

Avarenne grain prevents bad fruit years from becoming famine. Velisara offers ships, employment, and the temptation to leave. Seven Boughs supplies medicine and timber while sharing watersheds that no patrol can divide cleanly. A war at the verge would therefore reach city sickrooms, boatyards, and kitchen gardens long before it reached a formal battlefield.

## Open stories

A young duchess befriends the child of someone her regents intend to punish. An orchard family risks its oldest trees to shelter refugees during a blight. A forest guide and a harbour carpenter must bring storm timber home without destroying the slope below a village. A saint's popular shrine refuses a dangerous guest whom its founder's example seems to require it to admit. A turned young woman tries to preserve family life while facing hunger, coercion, and the need to choose responsibilities she can actually carry.
