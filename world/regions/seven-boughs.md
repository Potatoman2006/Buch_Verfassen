# The Seven Boughs

Seven Boughs is a western forest country of deep watersheds, inhabited clearings, tree-shadowed roads, flooded groves, and high canopies that make a cloudy noon resemble evening. Its seven names refer to old gathering regions, not seven literal trees or a single elven throne. The forest smells different with every elevation: wet leaf mould below, resin and fern on the slopes, cold lichen near the ridges. People who call it untouched have failed to notice centuries of careful human and nonhuman work.

## Houses under leaves

Nhaleth's resin verge contains Aelri and human orchard households, Thalen gatherers, Ruun potters, and communities whose grandparents arrived from Merenne. Houses stand in clearings and on stone-footed terraces. Few people live permanently high in trees; rope platforms serve watching, gathering, or ceremonial uses where a fall remains dangerous. Bough tongues vary between watersheds. Merennic and Low Meridian help at mixed markets without replacing a family's language of tenderness or anger.

Farther inland, the Stillwater communities use raised walkways between dry hummocks. Oshari families share the wet landscape with warm-blooded neighbours while needing different shelter and winter care. Their meals include river fish, roots, mushrooms, beans, and cultivated greens. Northern ridge settlements herd small animals and gather nuts. No one lives on mystical forest abundance. Stored food, imports, and the work of tending clearings remain essential.

Children learn to recognize bird alarm calls and to ask before cutting a branch another household has trained for a future use. Courtship can involve months of shared practical work followed by one conspicuously impractical gift. Festivals make clearings noisy: drums, masks, wrestling, cooking, and comic plays about self-important elders. Quiet woods do not imply a solemn people.

## The seven councils and the living guest

Watershed councils choose delegates to a seasonal gathering. Stewardship houses such as Nhaleth carry prestige because they preserve knowledge across generations, but their power varies. At baseline, Elder Asha Nhaleth wants to halt cutting on three saturated western slopes. Merenne's regents demand timber for sea defences. Asha remembers landslides that harbour envoys have never seen, yet underestimates the families living behind rotten walls.

The deeper internal conflict concerns Guest Bough communities whose ancestors arrived after Ash Summer or earlier wars. Some families have lived here longer than a human lifetime and still cannot speak fully in councils that govern their spring and paths. Older Aelri stewards say belonging requires patient learning beyond a short life. Human and Ruun residents ask how a standard no ordinary member of their people can meet can be called patience rather than exclusion. Thalen communities disagree among themselves, and some Aelri younger than the current council strongly support change.

An armed Old Canopy faction has begun dismantling roads used by outside cutters. Its supporters say peaceful refusal has failed. Families dependent on those same roads fear isolation during fever or famine. Conflict follows routes and households, not a pure forest people confronting a pure external world. Beyond this dispute, the Thorn Court has made a more terrible choice: to turn a hungry old forest into a border no human army can hold open cheaply.

## Faith as relation and argument

The Root Communion teaches that a living place contains relationships no single generation created. Its rites name the water, workers, animals, and dead involved in a meal. Some teachers believe the forest is a divine mind; others understand divinity as a relation among lives. Canon confirms neither a speaking forest god nor a universal shared consciousness.

Burials often return bodies to managed woodland away from drinking water. Families visit a place rather than expecting one tree to be the dead person. Guest Root congregations deliberately include newcomers in naming rites, which makes a religious meal politically consequential. Lamp houses at the western verge shelter lost travellers. Even their critics use them when a storm makes philosophical independence uncomfortable.

## The deep forest's beauty and teeth

The Glass Glade contains old Ilyran growth beds entwined with generations of roots. On cold nights, luminous fungi turn fallen branches blue-white, and a walker seems to pass between rivers of stars. Pilgrims sleep at its sheltered edge to wake with the light beneath them. Lovers knot pale grass there, and children search for the enormous moths that drink from its night flowers. The glade's beauty has its own place in a life, beyond whatever danger an author might bring into it.

Bell stags shelter in older woods. Near wound-exposed thickets, rare animals carry thorn growth among their antlers, spreading seeds when they fight or shed. Crown owls nest in hollow trunks large enough to shelter a family. Hearth drakes raid charcoal camps for warmth and scraps, their finite caustic spray dangerous to anyone who corners them. Forest guides avoid provoking these animals before reaching for weapons.

Growth workers can encourage a prepared cutting to root or guide a living hedge over months with soil, water, and learned patterns. Perception disturbances near certain old roots can repeat a familiar-looking path; travellers mark real bark, use ropes in difficult patches, and turn back when signs disagree. In the oldest wound-fed thickets, translucent stag shapes sometimes walk between the trunks alongside living animals, shedding soundless light from antlers broader than a doorway. These are local veil effects, but watching one pass can change how a frightened child remembers a journey for the rest of their life.

## The Briar-Wake and the Thorn Court

The Briar-Wake is an active web of aggressive thorn growth spreading through connected Ilyran beds beneath the northwestern forest. Fed by rain, rich soil, and the old prepared patterns, its leading edges can engulf several metres of clearing in weeks. It chokes paths, splits neglected walls, and draws bell stags whose thorn-seeded antlers spread it beyond the continuous root web. A farm can wake one spring with a living green wall between its house and fields. Cutting the visible hedge buys time; it does not remove roots working through an ancient bed.

The Thorn Court is a mixed Aelri, human, and Thalen militant fellowship led by Aelri former grove teacher Saevra Nhaleth, estranged from Elder Asha's house. Saevra lost her partner and an entire hillside settlement in a slide after illegal cutting. She now believes the inhabited verge must retreat before all seven watersheds are consumed by outside demand. Her followers plant rooted stakes into prepared ground and tend them until the Wake reaches selected roads. In a confrontation, a trained keeper can spend a mature hedge's stored growth to thrust a thorn curtain across a narrow path; the plants then brown and need recovery. There are roots, water, exhausted soil, and living practitioners behind each apparent miracle.

Saevra has ordered one occupied hamlet evacuated before sealing its road. Some residents escaped; others refused to leave winter stores and ancestral graves. Asha calls the act a betrayal of stewardship. Saevra calls the refusal to act a slower murder. Her closest human follower has family inside the threatened hamlet and has begun marking ways through the thorns at night. The conflict is immediate, personal, and supernatural, while remaining inseparable from the lives beneath the canopy.

## Places and neighbours

Nhaleth's gathering hall is built around a living tree without relying on that tree as its only support. Stillwater's night markets take place on linked platforms above frog-filled channels. The Glass Glade admits small groups in seasons when feet will do the least damage. The Rootless Court is an abandoned stone circle now used by Guest Bough families for their own assemblies; its name is an insult they have chosen to keep.

Merenne's walls, Kharad's furnaces, and Velisara's shipyards all ask something different of the same forest. Outside grain feeds several woodland settlements during poor nut years. Closing every border would harm the people it is meant to protect. Opening every grove would destroy the basis of that protection. The country lives inside that difficult middle without a magical answer.

## Open stories

A short-lived apprentice is asked to promise a century of service and must redefine what fidelity means. A Guest Bough funeral is refused access to a grove its family has tended for generations. Two guides escort rival timber crews through a fire-threatened watershed. A child returns from the Glass Glade carrying a harmless luminous fungus that becomes dangerous when cultivated badly indoors. An Old Canopy fighter shelters a wounded woodcutter and discovers that their convictions have never required listening this closely before.
