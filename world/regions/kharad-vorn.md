# Kharad-Vorn beneath the Crownspine

Kharad-Vorn joins mountain holds, glacial valleys, and halls carved close enough to the surface for food, air, and daylight to remain part of life. Its name evokes hot iron and resonant stone, but the first thing many visitors notice is the smell of bread. Kitchens open onto public galleries; water rings through channels beneath footbridges; whole neighbourhoods turn gold when reflected sunset enters a high shaft. The deep dark begins beyond the inhabited country, not at every doorstep.

## Two ways to belong to the mountain

Vorn Gate gathers Kharad families with humans, Urven hauliers, Ruun potters, and Redwind traders beneath an enormous weather-stained arch. Vornic predominates; Sarayi and Low Meridian are common at the surface markets. Its children know caravan songs and mountain planting festivals. Above the town, small farms grow hardy roots, cabbages, and barley. Goats browse ledges that visiting southerners refuse to call paths.

Emberwell's inner neighbourhoods are warmer, quieter, and more formal about unannounced visitors. Workshops surround fault-warmed baths; fungal gardens fill selected damp rooms without replacing the food grown outside. A family can live here for generations and still own a summer cottage in the valley. Belonging comes through apprenticeship, adoption, marriage, and rescue as well as descent. Old lineages sometimes pretend otherwise when status is at stake.

In both communities, a child's first successful repair is displayed at dinner regardless of how ugly it looks. Hospitality includes asking whether a guest wants the door open or shut. Kharad long lives make ancient grudges possible, but they also make exhausted reconciliation necessary. A feud cannot be sustained through a century merely by calling it tradition; someone must keep teaching children whom to hate.

## The shut crown

The Gate Assembly joins the major holds, while each hold keeps its own rulers and customs. The most prestigious old lineages trace descent from the companions of Queen Varud, who shut the eastern Deep Road during a subterranean war long before Saltfall. Their claim is cultural history; no preserved witness settles every companion's identity. Varud's crown is displayed empty above an unopened door, its absence from a living ruler's head a statement that no one hold owns all the mountain.

The present division concerns the Grey Descent, where recent tremors have exposed a stair into rooms associated with Varud's enemies. Lineage Vask wants a guarded expedition before strangers or desperate miners go down alone. Emberwell elders demand that the breach be filled, recalling family stories of bad air and creatures driven into the upper halls. Younger Kharad accuse them of using inherited fear to suppress discoveries that could overturn heroic genealogies. All three concerns can be true at once.

The holds also face refugees and herds arriving from Redwind's shortened grazing circuits. Surface villages welcome animals they can eat and fear losing winter hay. Avarenne offers grain in exchange for military passage. The Assembly's choice therefore reaches into both an unknown old descent and a very present contest at the gates.

## Faith in breath and fire

Forge Ancestor shrines keep worn tools, imperfect apprentice pieces, and the names of teachers who were not blood relatives. At a death, the household finishes one small thing the deceased left incomplete, then deliberately leaves another unfinished to acknowledge that a life cannot be tidied away. The Deep Covenant brings people together around shared water and air. Its most respected rite is admitting a dangerous mistake before somebody else suffers from it.

These practices do not make Kharad society uniformly honest. A lineage can cherish truthful work and lie fiercely about an ancestor. Common Hammer preachers argue that a living apprentice's hands deserve more respect than a dead master's title. Conservative teachers reply that humility before inherited skill keeps eager children alive. Their arguments unfold in kitchens and public baths as often as shrines.

## Wonders and dangers below

In the Mirror Vault, pale fungal fans grow across mineral veins and scatter lamplight into a field of stars. A wedding there takes place beneath another sky, while a thousand tiny blue reflections travel across the guests' faces. Far below, enormous pale moths rise from warm chambers in silent clouds. Their wings brush the faces of explorers like cool hands; a panicked swarm can fill a narrow passage with choking scales.

Stone listeners use mineral dust, measured vibration, and a learned pattern to read strain along prepared surfaces. They cannot see a person through a mountain or open arbitrary rock. Heat workers move existing warmth through fitted stone beds. A failed pattern can crack a vessel or leave a sleeping gallery dangerously cold. Such magic supports inhabited wonders while leaving agriculture, fuel, ventilation, and fatigue in force.

The Grey Descent contains old machinery, collapsed tomb passages, and chambers that repeat impacts from a long-ended battle. It also leads toward the hidden eastern surface basin of Skarn, whose inhabitants survived the old war through terraced farming, herding, and mountain routes absent from Vorn's familiar charts. They have neither slept for centuries nor ceased to change. Their ruling tradition calls Varud a usurper and treats the crown behind her shut door as stolen inheritance.

## The Mourning Host

Queen Nerez Vey, a Kharad ruler of Skarn, has sent the Mourning Host into the reopened Descent. Its members include Kharad, Urven, and human soldiers bound by a common homeland and incompatible private hopes. Their copper face masks carry prepared fragments of battlefield echoes through wet ceramic cartridges. For a few minutes within roughly twenty metres, an advancing company can fill a corridor with the sound and fleeting appearance of a battle larger than the living force. The cartridges require preparation at a scarred site and lose their effect when dry or broken. Terrified opponents still retain agency; physical soldiers with axes and shields stand behind the spectacle.

Nerez wants the crown, hostages from the upper lineages, and public submission to her version of history. Her first raiders have taken two small watch chambers and sent surviving defenders home carrying white funeral cloth. She believes she can win a limited campaign before winter closes the surface supplies. Her captain, the human Osa Rend, favours exchanging prisoners and bargaining from those gains. Nerez's adult son wants to march farther, convinced that restraint would repeat his ancestors' defeat. The Host has cooks, quarrelling households, conscientious soldiers, and cruel ones; it is dangerous because a living ruler has made conquest a sacred duty.

Vask's expedition proposal is now also a war decision. Several elders still speak as though sealing the Descent will end the danger, while young scouts report worked side passages. Other families demand revenge before learning whether missing kin remain alive. Nerez cannot conquer the Crownspine through sound alone, but the first returned survivors have already made the old crown feel less safely ceremonial.

## Places and relations

Vorn Gate's enormous bronze doors are opened for the first grass festival, when children carry green shoots inside. Emberwell's baths are places to heal, gossip, and hear foreign news stripped of official ceremony. The Widow's Needle rises above a pass where wind plays a natural flute through pierced stone. The sealed crown-door, now approached through a public gallery, remains a potent symbol even to people who do not believe its legends.

Rhydder sends masons to learn and brings back techniques altered for wet slate. Seven Bough charcoal and resin sustain crafts that the holds cannot feed from mountain scrub. Redwind supplies mounts, hides, and news from beyond the chart. Greyhaven buyers admire Kharad workmanship while often refusing to recognize the skill of the surface families who bring it to port. These frictions give the mountain a place in the world without making it a mere mine for other people's stories.

## Open stories

An adopted apprentice is asked to inherit a lineage's greatest craft and its cruelest secret. An expedition descends past Varud's sealed road while its members disagree about what counts as treasure. A valley household shelters Redwind strangers during a snow closure against the wishes of its neighbours. A master losing fine sensation in her fingers must decide whom to teach. A pilgrim comes to return a tool stolen by an admired ancestor and finds that nobody wants the simple apology he prepared.
