# The Nine Beacons

The Nine Beacons are southern lighthouse islands, salt towns, sheltered anchorages, and exposed rocks on which people have somehow built kitchens. From the sea, their lights seem like a promise. Ashore, that promise requires oil, climbing legs, clean lenses, food, and someone awake when everyone else needs sleep. The confederacy began after the Nine Fires of 73 IS, when surviving enemies accepted that a warning light had to serve ships whose flags they disliked.

## The islands within the islands

Pell is a substantial harbour island, crowded with Thalassic-speaking humans, Ruun, Serev, and Urven families. It has gardens, salt pans, boatyards, and a public school whose bell competes with the gulls. Smaller Skell has fewer than three hundred permanent inhabitants and a tower reached by hundreds of wet steps. Oshari families maintain nearby sheltered shallows and visit the market through a landing designed for both wet and dry arrivals.

Food comes from fish, legumes, island gardens, imported grain, and goats wherever goats can survive. Salt workers rise before the worst heat. Children learn knots and songs whose verses remember navigational features. A new verse can record a changed reef; an old favourite can become dangerously inaccurate. Islanders celebrate successful rescues with enormous meals, then quietly return to arguments suspended while the boats were out.

## Nine lights, unequal shadows

Each beacon community sends a representative to a seasonal gathering. Pell can provide more boats than several smaller islands combined, which gives its leader, Captain Ressa Pell, an influence formally equal voices cannot erase. Ressa wants armed escorts and a central refuge harbour. Skell's keeper, Dor Halven, fears the smaller lights will become expendable once every official ship shelters at Pell.

The argument has sharpened around the arrival of the White Keel, a scarred sailback leading a breeding pod through the outer approaches earlier than expected. These ocean reptiles can reach twenty to thirty-five metres. They are neither ships nor sea gods; a frightened adult can still break a wooden hull as completely as an enemy ram. Their presence has closed two customary passages and forced warships and grain vessels into the same narrow alternatives.

A foreign squadron wants permission to drive the pod away with fire boats. Pell's fishing households oppose the plan because injured animals may enter sheltered breeding water or turn against small craft. Dor refuses to lower his warning for any captain who chooses the dangerous channel. Ressa knows that denying the squadron may provoke a confrontation her islands cannot win easily. The great animal migration has become a military fact before any person chose to make it one.

## Return is a religious word

The Choir of Tides keeps the names of rescuers and rescued in communal songs, including people later disgraced. Erasing a person's crimes does not follow from remembering that someone once saved them. During the Night of Nine Fires, lights are lit in household windows inland as well as along shore. Families who lost someone may close their shutters without public shame.

Lamp shelters receive shipwreck survivors, including violent people temporarily unable to harm anyone. The difficult question begins when those survivors regain strength. Small islands have limited space to separate a dangerous guest from children and exhausted rescuers. Faith gives the conflict urgency, not an effortless answer.

## Light craft and open water

Beacon workers use polished metal, thick lenses, oil, and narrow heat and perception practices to steady and direct light. Magic improves a maintained flame; it does not burn without fuel or warn ships beyond a plausible horizon. Storm-darkened towers remain frightening even to someone who understands their workings. A keeper can spend a night hearing an unseen sea climb the rock beneath their home.

At the Bell Reef, mineral towers and old Ilyran fittings sound together under particular swells. The result travels like an enormous broken chord over the water. Pilots learn which notes usually accompany dangerous surge, while musicians come to listen for beauty that no performance can reproduce on demand. Glass eels gather in some warm channels around the reef and can injure a swimmer when disturbed in numbers.

## Places, relations, and stories

Pell's refuge harbour has a beach wide enough to draw up damaged boats. Skell's tower contains small living rooms between flights of stairs, every shelf secured against vibration. Bell Reef lies outside easy rescue distance in bad weather. The Empty Beacon is a storm-broken island whose former residents still visit to picnic, mourn, and argue about rebuilding.

Aurelian tables help navigation but cannot replace local judgement. Velisara supplies ships, oil, and impatient captains. Reef Crown families know passages that altered currents have made unfamiliar to tower keepers. An attack on one population's nursery can close another's food route; a rescue can turn a rival into a lifelong friend.

A keeper must keep a light burning while sheltering the person who killed a family member. Two rescue crews disagree over whether approaching a damaged warship will provoke the White Keel. A child raised inside a tower wants a life beyond its horizon. Survivors return to the Empty Beacon and find that their memories cannot agree on what should be rebuilt. A pilot refuses a captain's order and must face the cost of being right too late to make anyone grateful.
