# The peoples of the Inner Salt

Eight peoples are widely recognised around the Inner Salt, though travellers report other kinds of sapient life beyond the detailed map. Here a people means an inherited family of bodily traits, not a country, class or shared moral nature. “Race” is a common but imprecise everyday word. Names overlap with languages and countries because history is untidy: a Kharad person need not come from Kharad-Vorn, and a citizen of Kharad-Vorn need not be Kharad.

## Human lives

Humans occur throughout the region and display a broad range of skin tones, hair, stature and facial features. In safe conditions an ordinary natural life lasts roughly seventy to ninety years, sometimes longer. Violence, childbirth, disease and hard labour shorten many lives. Physical adulthood falls in the late teens; communities place civic adulthood anywhere from sixteen to twenty-five, with different expectations for marriage, military service and independent work.

Human adaptability is a description of widespread settlement, not an exclusive gift. Their dominance in many northern river districts follows fertile land, migration and states that encouraged numerous farming households. It does not follow a supernatural right to govern. Human communities include the orchard villages of Merenne, Sarayi-speaking pastoral families in Redwind, Crownspine miners and Oshari-speaking canal dwellers. Some people in those communities have never met a northern courtier and would resent being described as part of the same culture.

Among longer-lived neighbours, humans can find political patience indistinguishable from deliberate exclusion. An Aelri councillor's proposal to reconsider a matter in thirty years may consume most of a human petitioner's working life. Yet humans also benefit from inherited offices and wealth; short lives do not make a society open or egalitarian.

## Aelri, often called elves

Aelri are recognisably humanoid, usually long-limbed, with tapered ears and fine facial bone. Skin and hair vary considerably by ancestry. Their eyes adjust well to dim woodland light, but they cannot see in absolute darkness. Most reach physical adulthood between twenty-five and thirty; a healthy natural lifespan is approximately 260–360 years. Centuries of life leave visible changes: silvering hair, deepened facial lines, stiff joints and diminishing senses. They do not remain immaculate youths until a chosen moment of death.

An Aelri who remembers 73 IS is older but unexceptional in 250; a surviving witness of the Saltfall itself is elderly and much rarer. These living accounts preserve streets, weather and individual acts that shorter-lived neighbours know only from stories. A witness can describe the harbour where they stood without knowing what a distant commander intended. Losing children, spouses or human friends changes people differently, as do new attachments made across a long life.

Forest Aelri are especially visible in Seven Boughs, but coastal Aelri families have built ships for generations, and Avarenne has Aelri farming settlements whose inhabitants find court poetry embarrassing. Some support long apprenticeships as a safeguard against careless craft. Others argue that reserving a trade for sixty years of training protects comfortable masters. Young Aelri radicals may cooperate with human contemporaries who will become old before their shared movement achieves its aims.

## Kharad, often called dwarfs

Kharad are generally short, broad and strongly built, with dense bones and substantial hands. Facial hair varies by family and individual; it is neither universal nor a reliable sign of gender. They often reach physical adulthood in their mid-twenties and live 160–220 years under favourable conditions. Their eyes tolerate low light well. Dust scars their lungs as readily as anybody else's, and insufficient ventilation can kill a deep hall regardless of its builders' ancestry.

Many mountain settlements were founded by Kharad families because their ancestors developed ways to excavate and inhabit difficult rock. The techniques are teachable. A human mason can become excellent at them; a Kharad baker may know nothing about an arch. Coastal Kharad fishers, Redwind caravan households and forest smiths are longstanding communities, not curiosities requiring a recent explanation.

Kharad bodies retain heat well and often find southern summer labour punishing. Traditional clothing in warm ports leaves arms bare and uses broad ventilated hats; high-hall clothing favours layered felt. Those adaptations have sometimes become accusations of moral softness between cousins. Disputes between a mountain elder and a seaside grandchild are likelier to concern marriage, profession or where to live than whether either possesses an ancestral love of gold.

## Urven

Urven are broad-shouldered warm-blooded people, usually taller than neighbouring humans, with lower tusks, powerful jaw muscles and skin ranging from deep brown to olive, grey or muted green. Many live seventy-five to one hundred years. Physical maturity usually occurs around eighteen to twenty-two. Their tusks continue slowly changing through adulthood and require care; broken or diseased tusks can make eating painful. Their voices are capable of delicacy as well as volume.

Urven strength helps with some kinds of labour, but training, stature and health create wide variation. There is no inherited appetite for battle. Centuries of recruiting Urven into heavy infantry produced a military stereotype, then made peaceful migrants more likely to be offered military work. Some veteran families embrace martial traditions with pride; others want their children offered any profession at all.

Urven communities farm the Lume floodplain, herd in Redwind, work southern quays and cultivate steep orchard terraces in Merenne. Shared feast songs can link these distant communities without creating political unity. An Urven noble may exploit Urven tenants. An Urven pacifist may honour a warrior ancestor while rejecting that ancestor's example. Their useful story tensions come from those choices, not a struggle against supposed bad blood.

## Ruun

Ruun are small warm-blooded people, commonly between about one and one and a half metres tall. Fine fur covers much of the body, while faces and palms are largely bare. Mobile rounded ears and strong incisors are characteristic. Fur colour varies from cream and rust to brown, charcoal and black. They usually reach physical adulthood in the late teens and live sixty-five to eighty-five years. Their small bodies lose heat quickly, making shared sleeping rooms and layered clothing practical choices in cold country.

Ruun are common in market towns, hillside villages and shipboard communities. Compact homes may suit a family and cost less to heat, but small stature does not create a universal passion for underground houses. Many live in ordinary mixed streets with steps, work surfaces and handles adapted by their occupants. A port designed only for taller residents announces who its builders expected to matter.

They are frequently stereotyped as nimble, cheerful servants or cunning thieves. Neither image describes the Ruun soldier guarding a pass, the exhausted parent repairing nets, or the severe scholar whose lecture everybody dreads. Their reduced body mass affects medicines, carrying capacity and blood loss. Treating a smaller patient as a scaled-down healthy human without individual judgement has killed people. Vampires who exploit Ruun donors cannot evade the same physical consequence.

## Thalen

Thalen have humanoid bodies, mobile pointed ears, split nails that form hard paired tips, and branching antlers in most adult individuals. Antler growth differs substantially: some people carry modest forks, others broad crowns, and some have none. They shed and regrow them on a yearly cycle. Growing velvet is living, sensitive tissue; damaged antlers can bleed badly. Mature antler is not a magical organ. Thalen generally reach adulthood around twenty-five and live 110–150 years.

Their legs favour endurance on uneven ground, but they cannot run indefinitely or escape a horse on open country. Seasonal headgear, doors and sleeping arrangements accommodate changing antlers. In mixed cities, removable lintel panels and widened carriage hoods are ordinary crafts. Outsiders sometimes read antler size as rank; communities that actually do so are making a cultural choice, and plenty consider it vulgar.

Thalen live in northern uplands, forest fringes and Redwind river valleys. Some Seven Boughs houses follow careful antler burial practices, while coastal Thalen carvers turn shed antlers into tools and joke about relatives who disapprove. Human poets imagine them as instinctive guardians of untouched nature. A Thalen charcoal burner, sheep farmer or timber merchant knows how absurd that expectation is. Forest knowledge comes from teaching and attention, not the shape of one's head.

## Oshari

Oshari are amphibious, cold-blooded people with smooth mottled skin, broad hands and feet, protective inner eyelids and lungs supplemented by exchange through moist skin. They can remain underwater for about an hour under calm conditions, less when swimming hard or in poorly oxygenated water. They must still breathe air regularly. Clean moisture, suitable temperature and access to dry resting places shape their homes. Prolonged cold makes them slow and can become fatal. Their natural lifespan is about ninety to 120 years; maturation usually takes twenty to twenty-five years.

Warm estuaries, reed basins and sheltered southern lagoons support the largest Oshari populations. Heated bathhouses allow smaller northern communities. Their ancestors built underwater paths and fish nurseries that surface visitors often mistake for wilderness. Some villages practise seasonal inland migration when coastal waters turn too saline; others grow up on entirely freshwater rivers.

Oshari blood does not sustain vampires, whose sustaining food requires warm-blooded sapient donors. That fact offers no protection from ordinary violence or exploitation. Nor does living underwater give Oshari shared knowledge of every wreck. They employ divers, historians and pilots like everybody else. Outsider stories that describe them as inscrutable usually reveal how rarely the storyteller has tried to learn a language whose gestures remain visible below water.

## Serev

Serev are warm-blooded, feathered people with narrow beaked faces, grasping hands and strong digitigrade legs. Their forearms bear display feathers but do not form functional wings. **They cannot fly or glide.** Feather patterns vary dramatically, and moulting temporarily changes appearance. Many reach physical maturity around nineteen to twenty-two and live seventy to one hundred years. Lightweight bones do not make them fragile ornaments, but heavy crushing injuries are especially dangerous.

Serev voices carry a wide tonal range. Some languages use this ability extensively; a human speaker can learn them with an accent and occasional ambiguity. Fine hearing varies among individuals and is not a substitute for expertise. Crowded metal workshops are uncomfortable enough that Serev smiths often use padded ear coverings.

Serev households are prominent in southern island settlements, inland caravan towns and the cities of Velisara. Sailing professions suit some families through inheritance and opportunity, not a mystical kinship with the air. Inland Serev potters may never see the sea. Feather grooming is personal care, social intimacy or religious practice according to community; touching another person's plumage without invitation is not made acceptable by curiosity.

## Kinship across difference

Mixed households are common. Adoption, fosterage, marriage and sworn siblinghood often matter more than the possibility of shared biological children. Humans and Aelri can have children together; adult traits and longevity vary within a broad intermediate range, and families use the identities meaningful to them. Other combinations among these eight peoples do not ordinarily produce biological children. That fact has never prevented people from making families.

There is no dependable test for culture in blood. A child raised speaking Merennic, lighting the household lamp and working a cider orchard may feel entirely Merennic regardless of descent. A person can belong to several communities without solving the tension between them. Vampirism and other acquired conditions do not erase this history or create a ninth people. The word “monster” is something people apply to bodies, acts and fears; this atlas does not make their accusation an inherited truth.
